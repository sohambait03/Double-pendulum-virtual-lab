Theory

1. Introduction

A double pendulum is a mechanical system consisting of two pendulum links connected in series. The system has two independent angular coordinates and therefore possesses two degrees of freedom.

The double pendulum is an important example of a nonlinear coupled dynamic system and is commonly used to demonstrate concepts related to dynamics, vibrations, numerical methods, and chaotic motion.

2. System Configuration

The system consists of two masses \(m_1\) and \(m_2\), connected by links of lengths \(l_1\) and \(l_2\). The angular coordinates are measured from the vertically downward direction.

3. Degrees of Freedom

$$DOF = 2$$

$$q = \begin{bmatrix} \theta_1 \\ \theta_2 \end{bmatrix}$$

4. Assumptions

  • Links are rigid.
  • Masses are concentrated at the ends of the links.
  • Joints are frictionless.
  • Motion occurs in a single vertical plane.
  • Gravitational acceleration is constant.
  • Air resistance is neglected.
  • Links are assumed to have negligible mass.

5. Position Coordinates

Position of the first mass:

$$x_1 = l_1\sin\theta_1$$

$$y_1 = -l_1\cos\theta_1$$

Position of the second mass:

$$x_2 = l_1\sin\theta_1 + l_2\sin\theta_2$$

$$y_2 = -l_1\cos\theta_1 - l_2\cos\theta_2$$

6. Kinetic Energy

$$ T = \frac{1}{2}(m_1+m_2) l_1^2\dot{\theta}_1^2 + \frac{1}{2}m_2l_2^2 \dot{\theta}_2^2 $$

$$ + m_2l_1l_2 \dot{\theta}_1 \dot{\theta}_2 \cos(\theta_1-\theta_2) $$

7. Potential Energy

$$ V = -(m_1+m_2)gl_1\cos\theta_1 - m_2gl_2\cos\theta_2 $$

8. Lagrangian

$$ L = T - V $$

9. Lagrange's Equation

$$ \frac{d}{dt} \left( \frac{\partial L} {\partial \dot{q_i}} \right) - \frac{\partial L} {\partial q_i} =0 $$

10. Governing Differential Equations

The nonlinear equations of motion are:

$$ (m_1+m_2)l_1^2\ddot{\theta}_1 + m_2l_1l_2\ddot{\theta}_2 \cos(\theta_1-\theta_2) $$

$$ + m_2l_1l_2\dot{\theta}_2^2 \sin(\theta_1-\theta_2) + (m_1+m_2)gl_1\sin\theta_1 =0 $$

$$ m_2l_2^2\ddot{\theta}_2 + m_2l_1l_2\ddot{\theta}_1 \cos(\theta_1-\theta_2) $$

$$ - m_2l_1l_2\dot{\theta}_1^2 \sin(\theta_1-\theta_2) + m_2gl_2\sin\theta_2 =0 $$

11. Matrix Form

$$ \mathbf{M}(q)\ddot{q} + \mathbf{C}(q,\dot{q}) + \mathbf{G}(q) =0 $$

$$ \mathbf{M} = \begin{bmatrix} (m_1+m_2)l_1^2 & m_2l_1l_2\cos\Delta \\ m_2l_1l_2\cos\Delta & m_2l_2^2 \end{bmatrix} $$

where

$$ \Delta=\theta_1-\theta_2 $$

12. Numerical Solution

The governing differential equations are nonlinear and coupled. Therefore, the simulation solves them numerically using the fourth-order Runge-Kutta method.

$$\boxed{\text{RK4 Numerical Integration}}$$

Experimental Procedure

1

Set System Parameters

Enter suitable values of \(m_1\), \(m_2\), \(l_1\), and \(l_2\).

2

Set Initial Angles

Set the initial angular positions \(\theta_1\) and \(\theta_2\) using the sliders or input boxes.

3

Set Gravity

Set gravitational acceleration. The default Earth value is \(g=9.81\,m/s^2\).

4

Reset the Simulation

Click the Reset button to apply the selected initial conditions.

5

Start the Simulation

Click Start to begin the numerical integration and observe the motion of both pendulum masses.

6

Observe the Motion

Observe angular displacement, angular velocity and angular acceleration in real time.

7

Analyze the Graphs

Study the variation of \(\theta_1\), \(\theta_2\), \(\omega_1\), and \(\omega_2\) with time.

8

Check Energy Conservation

Compare the initial and current total mechanical energy to evaluate numerical accuracy.

About the Virtual Laboratory

About This Project

The Double Pendulum Virtual Laboratory is an interactive educational simulation developed to demonstrate the dynamic behavior of a nonlinear two-degree-of- freedom mechanical system.

The governing equations are derived using Lagrangian mechanics and solved numerically using the fourth-order Runge-Kutta method.

Model Information

  • System: Double Pendulum
  • Degrees of Freedom: 2
  • Mathematical Model: Nonlinear
  • Numerical Solver: RK4
  • Coordinate System: Absolute Angular Coordinates
  • Motion: Planar
  • Gravity: 9.81 m/s²
